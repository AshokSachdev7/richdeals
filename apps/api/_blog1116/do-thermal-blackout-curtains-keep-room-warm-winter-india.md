# Do Thermal Blackout Curtains Keep a Room Warm in Winter? (India Guide)

**Short answer:** Yes, but only a little, and only if they are fitted well. A lined thermal or blackout curtain traps a layer of still air between the fabric and the cold glass, which cuts the heat a room loses through its windows at night. It will not heat a room on its own and it will not replace a heater or a good blanket. In a north Indian winter it is a cheap way to make the heat you already have last longer.

Most Indian homes were built for summer, not winter. Single-pane glass, aluminium sliding frames with gaps at the rails, and big bedroom windows are normal from Delhi to Lucknow to Jaipur. When the outside temperature falls to single digits at night, that glass becomes the coldest surface in the room. This guide explains what thermal curtains actually do, where they help, where they do not, and how to hang them so they are worth the money. ## How a room loses heat through a window

Heat leaves a warm room through a window in three ways:

- **Conduction through the glass.** Warm room air touches the cold pane, cools, and sinks. Single glazing is a poor insulator, so this is usually the biggest loss.
- **Drafts through gaps.** Sliding windows and old wooden frames leak cold air around the edges. You feel this as a chill near the floor.
- **Radiation.** Your body and the warm objects in the room radiate heat toward the cold glass. This is why sitting next to a window feels cold even when the room thermometer reads fine.

How much of a home's heat goes out through windows depends on the building. A figure often quoted and attributed to the US Department of Energy is around 30% in cold weather, while some UK sources put it nearer 18%. Neither was measured in Indian homes, so treat them as a rough sense of scale, not a promise.

## What a thermal curtain actually does

A thermal curtain is a thick, tightly woven or multi-layer fabric, often with a foam or coated backing. A blackout curtain is built to block light, but the same dense backing also slows heat. Most budget curtains sold in India as "thermal" are blackout curtains with a coated back.

When drawn, the curtain does two useful things:

1. **It creates a pocket of trapped air** between itself and the glass. Still air is a decent insulator, so less room heat reaches the cold pane.
2. **It blocks radiation.** You stop "feeling" the cold window, which is why a room with closed heavy curtains feels warmer even at the same air temperature.

Curtain sellers claim thermal curtains cut window heat loss by 25% or even 40%. Those numbers come from manufacturers and retailers, not independent tests, and the real result depends on the fabric, the lining and above all the fit. A loose curtain that stops well above the floor and leaves gaps at the sides does very little.

## Thermal vs blackout vs regular curtains

| Curtain type | Blocks light | Slows heat loss | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sheer / net | No | Almost none | Daytime privacy |
| Regular cotton or polyester | Partly | A little | Living rooms |
| Blackout (coated back) | Mostly or fully | Moderate | Bedrooms, day sleepers |
| Thermal (multi-layer or foam-backed) | Usually fully | Best of the four | Cold rooms, big windows |

For winter warmth, weight and density matter more than the word printed on the box. A heavy blackout curtain with a coated back will usually beat a thin curtain labelled "thermal". Dark colours absorb a bit more sunlight during the day, but at night the backing matters far more than the colour.

## Where thermal curtains help most in India

- **North India and hill areas** (Delhi NCR, Punjab, Haryana, UP, Rajasthan nights, Uttarakhand, Himachal): night temperatures drop low enough that window heat loss is real.
- **Bedrooms with large windows or glass balcony doors**, especially on north-facing walls that never get direct sun.
- **Rented flats** where you cannot change the windows. A curtain rod is a far smaller change than new glazing.
- **Rooms where you run a heater.** A room heater or oil-filled radiator works less hard when less heat escapes. Read our guide on [room heater safety and dry air](/blog/does-room-heater-reduce-oxygen-dry-air-safety-tips-india) before you run one all night.

In Mumbai, Chennai, Bengaluru or Kolkata, winter nights rarely get cold enough for this to matter. There the same blackout curtain earns its place in summer by keeping afternoon sun out and helping the AC.

## How to hang them so they actually work

Fit decides whether a thermal curtain is useful or decorative:

1. **Go wide.** The rod should extend 10-15 cm past the window frame on each side so the curtain overlaps the wall, not just the glass. Our [curtain size guide](/blog/curtain-size-guide-5-ft-7-ft-9-ft-panels-per-window-india) covers panel counts and lengths for standard 5, 7 and 9 ft curtains.
2. **Go long.** The curtain should reach the sill, or touch the floor for doors. A gap at the bottom lets cold air spill out under it.
3. **Close the top.** Mount the rod close to the ceiling or use a pelmet. Warm air rises, and an open top lets it circulate behind the curtain onto the glass.
4. **Use two panels that overlap in the middle** rather than one panel that barely meets the edge.
5. **Seal drafts first.** Stick self-adhesive foam strip along sliding window rails. A curtain cannot fix a gap that blows cold air straight in.

## When to open and close them

- **Open them on sunny winter days**, especially on south- and west-facing windows. Free sunlight is the cheapest heater you have.
- **Close them before sunset**, not after you start feeling cold. Glass loses heat fast once the sun drops, and closing early keeps the day's warmth inside.
- **Keep them closed overnight** in bedrooms. That is when it is coldest outside and the curtain does the most work.
- **Air the room briefly each morning.** Closed curtains plus closed windows trap moisture, and condensation on cold glass can lead to mould on the curtain backing.

## What thermal curtains will not do

- They will not raise a cold room's temperature by themselves. They only slow the loss of heat you already have from people, sunlight or a heater.
- They will not stop cold coming through walls, the floor or an open door.
- They do little if hung loosely, short, or with gaps at the sides.

## Buying checklist

Before you order, check:

- **Backing:** coated, foam-lined or triple-weave. Hold the fabric up to a bulb; if you can see the bulb clearly, it will not insulate much.
- **Size:** measure the window width plus 20-30 cm, and the drop from rod to sill or floor.
- **Header:** eyelet curtains slide easily and close fully; loop headers often leave gaps.
- **Washability:** coated backs can crack in a hot wash. Check the care label.
- **Count:** most windows need two panels, so compare price per pair, not per piece.

## FAQ

**Do blackout curtains keep heat in?**
Partly. The dense coated backing that blocks light also slows heat leaving through the glass. A dedicated multi-layer thermal curtain does it better, but a heavy blackout curtain is a clear step up from a regular one.

**Are thermal curtains worth it in India?**
In north India and hill areas, yes, as a cheap add-on, especially in bedrooms with large windows or in rented homes. In coastal and southern cities they are more useful for blocking summer heat than winter cold.

**Should thermal curtains touch the floor?**
For a window, reaching the sill is enough; for a balcony door, they should touch or nearly touch the floor. Any gap at the bottom lets cold air spill into the room.

**Which side of a thermal curtain faces the window?**
The coated or foam backing faces the glass, and the decorative fabric faces the room.
