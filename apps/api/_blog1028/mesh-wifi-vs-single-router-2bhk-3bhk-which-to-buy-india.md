# Mesh Wi-Fi vs Single Router: Which to Buy for a 2BHK or 3BHK in India

**Mesh Wi-Fi vs a single router comes down to walls, not square feet.** A compact 2BHK with the router near the middle is usually fine on one good Wi-Fi 6 router. A 3BHK, a long L-shaped flat or one with thick concrete walls is where a 2-pack mesh system (also sold as a whole-home Wi-Fi kit) starts paying for itself. Below: how to decide in two minutes, what the budget mesh sets on our deals pages cost right now, and the setup mistakes that make a mesh feel no better than the router it replaced.

## The 2-minute decision

- **One router is enough when:** the flat is a compact 1-2BHK, the router can sit near the centre, and most devices are in two or three rooms.
- **Go mesh when:** you have a 3BHK or duplex, the router has to live in a corner near the ISP's fibre entry point, or there is a dead room (usually the farthest bedroom or the balcony study).
- **Neither fixes a slow plan.** If your broadband is 100 Mbps, a ₹8,000 mesh will not make it 300. It only stops the signal dying before it reaches the room.

## Why concrete walls decide this

Indian flats are built with reinforced concrete and brick, and 5 GHz Wi-Fi, the band that gives the fast speeds, loses far more strength through walls than the older 2.4 GHz band. That is why a single router can show full bars in the hall and a weak signal two rooms away. A mesh puts a second node in the weak zone, so your phone talks to a nearby node instead of fighting through walls. Floor plans matter more than the carpet-area number on the brochure: a 1,000 sq ft open layout can be easier to cover than a 700 sq ft flat with four small rooms.

## The three setup mistakes that ruin a mesh

1. **Putting the second node too far away.** Place it roughly halfway to the dead room, not inside it. The node needs a decent signal from the first one to pass it on. If your phone shows only one or two bars next to the second node, it is too far.
2. **Leaving the ISP router doing its own Wi-Fi.** Many fibre routers from Indian ISPs broadcast their own Wi-Fi and run their own network. Plug the mesh main node into it and you can end up with a "double NAT" setup, which breaks some games and CCTV apps. Switch the ISP box to bridge or access-point mode if its menu allows it, or ask your ISP to enable bridge mode. If it does not, keep it and disable its Wi-Fi radios instead.
3. **Ignoring cables.** Where you can, connect nodes with an Ethernet cable ("wired backhaul"). Mesh nodes talk to each other over Wi-Fi by default, and that uses part of the very bandwidth you are trying to deliver. A wired link removes that penalty. If your flat has conduits, a 20 m [Cat5e patch cable](/atevon-20m-cat5-cat5e-high-speed-ethernet-lan-cable-rj45-network-patch-cord-for) costs about ₹230 and a 50 m [Cat6 roll](/fedus-50-meter-cat6-ethernet-lan-cable-b08dlwl2gh) about ₹932 on our pages. Check that the nodes you buy have a spare LAN port first.

A router that dies in a power cut takes your Wi-Fi with it. ## Quick buying checklist

- **Flat size and layout:** one router for compact, central; mesh for anything long or multi-floor.
- **Plan speed:** match the gear to your plan, and check what the ISP supports.
- **Device count:** more than about 15 devices points to Wi-Fi 6.
- **LAN ports:** needed if you want wired backhaul or to connect a TV or CCTV recorder.
- **Warranty and service:** buy from the brand's authorised seller; for a mesh, the app support matters as much as the hardware.

## FAQ

**Is mesh Wi-Fi worth it for a 2BHK?**
Usually not, if the router sits near the middle. It is worth it if the router is stuck in a corner, if the walls are thick, or if one bedroom keeps losing signal. Try moving the router to a central, raised spot first; that costs nothing.

**Can I use a mesh system with my ISP's router?**
Yes. Connect the mesh main node to the ISP router's LAN port, and where possible set the ISP router to bridge mode so only the mesh creates the network. Otherwise you may see double NAT.

**Is a range extender the same as mesh?**
No. An extender repeats an existing signal, often under a second network name and at reduced speed. A mesh is one system, with one network name, designed to hand your device from node to node.

**Do I need Wi-Fi 6 for a 100 Mbps plan?**
No, but it helps when many devices are connected at once. If you are buying new today, the price gap to Wi-Fi 6 is small on the deals above, so it is the safer pick.

**How many nodes do I need for a 3BHK?**
Two is the usual starting point. Add a third only if a dead room remains after you place the second node properly.
