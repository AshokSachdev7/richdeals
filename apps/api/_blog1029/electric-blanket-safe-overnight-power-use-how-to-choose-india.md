# Is an Electric Blanket Safe Overnight? Power Use, Cost and How to Choose (India)

**Short answer:** a modern electric blanket is generally safe to sleep with overnight *if* it meets four conditions:
- it has an auto shut-off timer and overheat protection;
- it carries an ISI mark;
- its wiring is undamaged;
- you follow the maker's instructions.

The safer habit is to preheat the bed for 20-30 minutes, then drop to the lowest setting or let the timer switch it off. On power, most single-bed electric blankets sold in India are rated somewhere around 50-70 W and doubles around 100-150 W. A full night costs a few rupees, a fraction of what a room heater burns.

This guide covers:
- who should not use one;
- what an electric blanket really costs to run per night;
- how it compares with a room heater and a hot water bag;
- a checklist for buying one this winter.

## Is an electric blanket safe to sleep with all night?

Electric blankets use thin, insulated heating wires sewn into the fabric. A plug-in controller sets the heat level. People worry about fire, shocks and burns. Those risks mostly come from three things:
- **old or damaged wiring;**
- **folding or bunching while it is on;**
- **models with no automatic cut-off.**

A blanket in good condition with these features is designed for overnight use:

- **Auto shut-off timer:** switches the heat off after a set time. Listings in India range from about 2 hours to 12 hours, so read the spec sheet rather than assume.
- **Overheat protection:** cuts power if a section of the wire gets too hot, for example where the blanket is folded.
- **Detachable controller with clear heat levels:** lets you run it on low and makes the blanket easier to clean.
- **Explicit "safe for overnight use" wording** in the manual. If the maker says pre-heat only, treat it as pre-heat only.

One India-specific point: in many areas, power cuts and voltage swings are common. When the supply returns while you are asleep, a blanket without a proper controller may come back on at full heat. That is another reason to insist on an auto-off model and keep it on its lowest setting overnight.

### Who should not use an electric blanket

Skip it, or ask a doctor first, for:

- **Babies, toddlers and anyone who cannot switch it off on their own.**
- **People with diabetes, nerve damage (neuropathy) or poor circulation**, who may not feel when the skin is getting too hot.
- **Pregnant women**, because raising core body temperature is best avoided.
- **Anyone with bladder control problems**, because liquid and live wires do not mix.

For these sleepers, preheat the bed and **switch the blanket off before getting in**, or use a plain heavy blanket instead.

## Electric blanket power consumption: what one night costs

Electricity use is simple arithmetic: **watts × hours ÷ 1000 = units (kWh).** Your bill charges per unit, and the rate depends on your state and slab. The table below uses an example rate of ₹8 per unit. Swap in the figure printed on your own bill.

| Appliance | Typical rating | 8 hours of use | Approx. cost per night (at ₹8/unit) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single electric blanket | ~60 W | 0.48 units | about ₹4 |
| Double electric blanket | ~120 W | 0.96 units | about ₹8 |
| Carbon room heater | ~950 W | 7.6 units | about ₹61 |
| Fan or halogen heater | ~2,000 W | 16 units | about ₹128 |

Two things push the blanket's real cost even lower:
- most of the time it runs on a low setting rather than full power;
- the auto-off timer often cuts it after a few hours.

Over a 30-night month, a single blanket used every night costs roughly ₹120 or less at that rate. An all-night room heater can add four figures to the bill.

## How to choose an electric blanket in India

**1. Under-blanket or over-blanket.** Most electric blankets sold here are **under-blankets**, also called bed warmers. They go on the mattress, under the bedsheet. They are the common choice because heat rises into the bed and the wires sit flat. An over-blanket is used on top like a throw. It needs extra care so it never gets bunched up.

**2. Size.**
- A single is roughly 120-150 cm wide and suits one sleeper.
- A double is around 150-180 cm wide.

Measure your mattress before ordering. On a shared double bed, a dual-control model lets each side set its own heat. These are rare in India, so most couples buy a double with one controller.

**3. Wattage.** Higher wattage heats faster but is not "better". For a single bed, a blanket in the 50-70 W range is plenty. For a double, around 100-150 W. Check the rating on the product page or label.

**4. Safety features first.** In order of importance:
1. auto shut-off;
2. overheat protection;
3. an ISI mark;
4. a solid, detachable controller with at least two or three heat levels.

A blanket missing the first two should not go on your shortlist.

**5. Washability.** Look for "machine washable" or "hand washable with controller removed". Otherwise you are limited to spot cleaning.

**6. Warranty.** Wiring is the part that wears out, so a longer warranty on the heating element is worth paying a little extra for.

## Safe-use checklist for every night

- **Preheat, then lower:** switch on 20-30 minutes before bed, then turn it to low or off.
- **Never fold, roll or tuck it tightly** while it is on, and do not sleep on a crumpled section.
- **Nothing heavy on top:** no suitcases, pets or stacked pillows over the heated area.
- **No pins.** Safety pins or needles can damage the wires.
- **Do not combine** it with a hot water bag in the same bed.
- **Keep the controller out of the bedding**, where it can stay cool and dry.
- **Inspect it every winter** before first use. Look for scorch marks, exposed wire, a frayed cord or a loose plug. Any of these means replace it; do not repair it.
- **Store it loosely rolled**, not sharply folded, in summer.

## FAQ

**Can I leave an electric blanket on all night?**
Only if the manual says it is approved for overnight use and it has auto shut-off and overheat protection. Even then, run it on the lowest setting. Preheating and switching off is the safest habit.

**How much electricity does an electric blanket use?**
Most single-bed models in India are rated around 50-70 W and doubles around 100-150 W. Eight hours at 60 W uses about 0.48 units. That is a few rupees per night at typical domestic tariffs.

**Is an electric blanket better than a room heater?**
For staying warm in bed, yes: it uses a small fraction of the power and does not dry the room air. A room heater is better when you need the whole room warm while awake.

**Do electric blankets cause health problems?**
For healthy adults, a blanket in good condition used as directed is generally considered safe. Some people should avoid sleeping with one switched on:
- people with diabetes or neuropathy;
- pregnant women;
- young children.

**How long does an electric blanket last?**
It depends on use and care, but the wiring is what fails first. Inspect it every season and replace it at the first sign of damage, scorching or an unreliable controller.
