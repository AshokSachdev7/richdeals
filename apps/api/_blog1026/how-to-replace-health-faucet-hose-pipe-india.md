# How to Replace a Health Faucet Hose Pipe at Home (India Guide)

**Short answer:** to replace a health faucet hose pipe, close the angle valve (or the main tap), unscrew the hose nut at the wall end and the gun end by hand or with a spanner, fit the new hose with its rubber washers seated flat, hand-tighten both nuts and add a quarter turn, then open the water and check for drips. Standard Indian bathroom fittings use a 1/2-inch BSP thread, so almost any 1-metre or 1.5-metre SS304 hose fits. It takes about 10 minutes and a new hose costs around ₹200-350.

Most people call a plumber for a job that needs no special tools. This guide covers how to tell whether the hose is the problem, what to buy, and the exact steps for Indian fittings.

## Is it the hose, the gun or the valve?

Before buying anything, find where the water is coming from. A health faucet (jet spray, bidet spray) has three parts that can leak, and only one of them is fixed by a new hose.

| Where it drips | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| From the steel coil along the hose, or the hose bulges | Inner tube has split | Replace the hose |
| From the nut at either end of the hose | Washer worn, flattened or missing | New washer first; hose if the crimped collar is loose |
| From the spray head when the trigger is not pressed | Gun valve or seal worn | Replace the gun, or buy a full set |
| From the knob or spindle on the wall valve | Angle valve worn | Replace the angle valve |
| From behind the valve, at the wall | Pipe joint inside the wall | Call a plumber; do not force it |

A quick test: close the angle valve and dry everything with a cloth. Open it slowly and watch for 30 seconds without pressing the trigger. Water on the hose body or at a nut means hose or washer. Water only at the spray head means the gun.

## What you need

- **A replacement hose.** A 1-metre hose suits most Indian WCs with the valve beside the seat. Choose 1.5 metres if the valve is on the far wall or you also use the spray for cleaning the floor.
- **Two rubber washers.** New hoses come with them fitted inside the nuts. Keep one or two spares, they cost a few rupees at any hardware shop.
- **Teflon (PTFE) tape**, optional. It goes on the male thread of the angle valve, not on the washer.
- **An adjustable spanner**, only if the old nut is stuck. Wrap the jaws in a cloth so you do not scratch the chrome.
- **A bucket or mug** for the water left in the line.

## Step-by-step: replacing the hose

### 1. Shut off the water

Turn the knob on the angle valve that feeds the health faucet clockwise until it stops. If there is no separate valve, or it no longer closes fully, shut the bathroom's main tap or the overhead tank line. Press the spray trigger once to release the pressure in the hose.

### 2. Unscrew the old hose at the wall end

Hold the bucket under the joint and turn the hose nut anticlockwise. Most come off by hand. If it is stuck with hard-water scale, use the cloth-wrapped spanner and keep the other hand on the valve so you do not twist it in the wall.

### 3. Unscrew the gun end

The other end of the hose screws into the spray gun. Unscrew it the same way. If you are keeping the old gun, look inside its inlet and remove any old washer stuck there.

### 4. Clean the threads

Wipe the valve's thread with a dry cloth. White scale comes off with an old toothbrush dipped in vinegar. A clean thread lets the new nut seat straight and stops cross-threading.

### 5. Check the washers and add tape if you want

Look into each nut of the new hose. A flat black rubber washer should be sitting evenly inside. This washer does the sealing, not the thread. If you want extra protection on an old valve thread, wrap 3-4 turns of Teflon tape clockwise (looking at the end of the thread), so tightening the nut does not unwind it. Jaquar's own health faucet installation guide tells installers to apply Teflon tape to threaded joints before tightening.

### 6. Fit the gun end, then the wall end

Screw the hose onto the gun first, hand-tight. Then start the wall-end nut on the valve thread by hand. It should spin freely for two or three turns; if it binds immediately, it is cross-threaded, so back it off and start again.

### 7. Tighten correctly

Hand-tight plus about a quarter turn with the spanner is enough. Overtightening squashes the washer out of shape, which causes the very leak you are fixing, and can crack an ABS gun's inlet.

### 8. Open the water and test

Open the angle valve slowly. Watch both nuts for a minute, then press the trigger a few times and check again. A slow drip at a nut usually needs an eighth of a turn more, or a washer that is reseated flat.

## Which replacement hose to buy

Nearly every health faucet hose sold in India is a stainless steel interlocking coil over a flexible inner tube, with a 1/2-inch nut at each end. Indian angle valves and bib cocks are sold with 1/2-inch BSP threads, as listings from brands like Kimson show, which is why hoses from different brands are interchangeable.

| Feature | What to look for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Outer coil | SS304 stainless steel | Resists rust in a wet bathroom; cheaper coils pit and stain |
| Inner tube | EPDM rubber, ideally nylon-braided | The inner tube holds the pressure; the steel coil only protects it |
| Length | 1 m for most WCs, 1.5 m for far-wall valves | Too short pulls on the joints; too long drags on the floor |
| End nuts | Brass or chrome-plated brass | Plastic nuts strip if overtightened |
| In the box | Washers already fitted | Saves a trip to the hardware shop |

Brand kits list the same parts. Ruhe's health faucet sets, for example, ship with a 304-grade stainless steel hose, an ABS holder, two screws and two wall plugs.

## Hose only or the full set?

If the gun sprays cleanly and does not drip when released, buy only the hose. If the gun is yellowed, has a sticky trigger or drips from the head, a full set often costs only ₹100-200 more than a hose alone and replaces everything that wears.

ABS guns do not rust and are light, which is why most budget sets use them. Brass guns feel heavier and are common in premium sets. For a leak problem the body material matters less than the hose and washers, because that is where nearly all health faucet leaks start.

## How to make the new hose last longer

- **Hang it on the hook, coiled loosely.** Sharp bends at the nut split inner tubes first.
- **Close the angle valve when you travel** so the hose is not under line pressure for days.
- **Keep it out of the WC bowl.** Cleaning acid corrodes the coil.
- **Descale the spray head** in hard-water cities with diluted vinegar.

## FAQ

### What size is a health faucet hose in India?

Almost all are 1/2 inch at both ends, matching the 1/2-inch BSP thread of standard Indian angle valves and bib cocks. Length is the only real choice, usually 1 m or 1.5 m.

### Do I need Teflon tape on a health faucet hose?

Not strictly. The rubber washer inside the nut makes the seal. A few turns of Teflon tape on an old or slightly worn valve thread adds protection and is cheap, but it does not replace a missing washer.

### Why does my new health faucet hose leak at the nut?

Usually the washer is missing, twisted or squashed from overtightening, or the nut is cross-threaded. Unscrew it, reseat the washer flat, start the thread by hand and tighten only a quarter turn past hand-tight.

### How often should a health faucet hose be replaced?

There is no fixed interval. Replace it when the coil rusts, the hose bulges, it drips along its length or the crimped ends loosen. Hard water and constant pressure shorten its life, so closing the angle valve when you are away helps.

### Can I fit any brand's hose to my existing health faucet?

In most cases yes, because Indian guns and valves share the 1/2-inch thread. Check the inlet on your gun before buying; a few designer or imported sets use their own fittings.
